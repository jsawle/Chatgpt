Earthquake Theatre

This rebuild is a self-contained 3D model with a regional curved-Earth cutaway. It uses Cartesian geometry projected into a rotatable view, not a flat map or a pre-rendered picture. Globe context is a schematic Earth, not satellite imagery. The cutaway removes the foreground to expose interior paths.

Teach in three chapters

  1. Watch: play, pause at arrivals, rotate the cutaway and compare P/S fronts.
  2. Measure: click Pick P or Pick S, then the corresponding trace; numeric inputs support keyboard entry.
  3. Solve: add station constraints sequentially, then calculate the focus and its depth.

Science

All events, stations, waveforms and times are synthetic. The Earth radius is 6371 km. The source is at 39° N, 10° E and 120 km depth. Stations retain the previous coordinates. P=6 km/s and S=3.5 km/s are illustrative constants. Straight chord distance d generates tP=d/6 and tS=d/3.5. Therefore d=(S−P)/(1/3.5−1/6). Subtraction cancels the common origin-time offset.

Three sphere equations produce two candidate positions. A fourth distance from East Point selects the better-fitting candidate in the 0–700 km teaching range. Depth = Earth radius minus the focus's distance from the centre. The surface epicentre is the radial projection of the focus. The calculation is geometric, not a noisy-data least-squares locator; it does not compute confidence intervals.

Fronts are expanding spheres in a homogeneous medium, clipped to Earth in the cutaway. Only selected wireframe arcs are drawn. Dots show progress along direct paths, not rock particles. Real waves refract, reflect and depend on layered Earth velocities. Depth phases such as pP provide additional depth evidence but are not simulated. This model does not reproduce core behaviour or seismic shadow zones.
